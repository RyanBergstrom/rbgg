# Lost Cities: Complete Rules Guide

Welcome to the definitive rules guide for **Lost Cities**, the classic two-player card game of calculated risk and exploration designed by Reiner Knizia. 

---

## 1. Overview & Theme
For the daring and adventurous, there are many lost cities to find and explore across five unique destinations:
* **Himalayas**
* **Brazilian Rain Forest**
* **Desert** (ever-shifting sands)
* **Volcanoes** (ancient volcanoes)
* **Neptune's Realm** (underwater)

Because resources are limited, players must carefully choose which expeditions to begin and which to leave to their opponent. Finding the right balance between high-risk investments and steady progress is the key to victory.

---

## 2. Game Components
* **1 Game Board**: Features spaces for the five destinations (both for player expeditions and discard piles).
* **60 Cards**:
  * **45 Expedition Cards**: 9 cards numbered **2 through 10** for each of the 5 destinations.
  * **15 Investment Cards**: 3 investment cards (handshake symbols) for each of the 5 destinations.

---

## 3. Preparation
1. **Board Placement**: Place the game board between the two players.
2. **Rounds**: Decide how many rounds to play (**three is a recommended standard**). Keep paper and pencil handy to track scores across rounds.
3. **Dealing**: Shuffle all 60 cards thoroughly and deal **8 cards** face down to each player as their starting hand.
4. **Draw Pile**: Place the remaining cards face down near the board to form the draw pile.
5. **Looking at Hands**: Players may look at their starting hands, but must keep them hidden from their opponent.

---

## 4. Playing the Game
The **oldest player begins**, and turns alternate back and forth between players. 

On your turn, you must perform two steps in strict order:
1. **Play one card** from your hand.
2. **Draw one card** to replace it.

### Step 1: Play a Card
When playing a card from your hand, you have two choices: **Start/Extend an Expedition** or **Discard a Card**.

#### Option A: Start or Extend an Expedition
You may only start or add to expeditions on **your side** of the board. 

* **Starting a New Expedition:** Place a card face up next to the space on the board corresponding to that destination.
* **Extending an Expedition:** Place a new card so that it partially covers the previous card in that expedition, ensuring all previous card values remain visible.
* **Ascending Order Rule:** Backtracking is not allowed. Successive cards added to an expedition must advance toward its goal using **increasingly higher numbers (2 through 10)**. The numbers do not need to be consecutive, but any later card must have a strictly higher value than the previous card.
* **Investment Cards:** Each destination has 3 investment cards. 
  * They **must** be played at the *very beginning* of an expedition before any numbered cards (2–10) are played.
  * Once a numbered card is played to an expedition, **no more investment cards** may be added to it.
  * Playing investment cards increases both your potential reward and your risk/penalty if the expedition fails.

#### Option B: Discard a Card
Instead of playing to an expedition, you may discard a card.
* Place the card face up on the board in the designated discard space matching that destination.
* If there are already cards in that discard pile, place the new card on top so that it covers previous cards. **Only the top card** of each destination's discard pile is fully visible and available for drawing.

---

### Step 2: Draw a Card
After playing your card (to an expedition or a discard pile), you must draw a replacement card into your hand. You have two sources to choose from:
1. **The Draw Pile:** Take the top card face down from the main draw pile.
2. **A Discard Pile:** Take the top card from any of the five destination discard piles.
   * **Restriction:** You **may not** immediately draw the exact card you just discarded on that same turn.

---

## 5. Game End & Scoring

### Triggering the End of a Round
The round ends **immediately** when a player draws the final card from the draw pile. Players are permitted to count the remaining cards in the draw pile during the game to plan their moves.

### Calculating Scores
Each player scores their expeditions separately according to the following steps:

1. **Sum the Numbered Cards:** Add together the values of all numbered cards (2–10) in the expedition.
2. **Subtract Base Cost:** Subtract **20 points** from the sum to represent the base cost and overhead of mounting the expedition. 
   * *Exception:* If you played **no cards at all** to an expedition, its value is **0** instead of -20.
   * *Investment-Only Expedition:* If an expedition contains only investment cards and no numbered cards, its value is considered **-20**.
3. **Apply Multipliers:** Multiply the resulting expedition value (whether positive or negative) by **1 plus the number of investment cards** you played on that expedition:
   * **0 Investment cards:** Multiplier is $\times 1$ (Value remains unchanged)
   * **1 Investment card:** Multiplier is $\times 2$
   * **2 Investment cards:** Multiplier is $\times 3$
   * **3 Investment cards:** Multiplier is $\times 4$
   * *(Note: An investment-only expedition with 3 investment cards results in $-20 \times 4 = -80$ points!)*
4. **Add Length Bonus:** If an expedition contains **at least 8 cards** (counting both investment cards and numbered cards combined), it earns a fixed **+20 point bonus**, which is added *after* the multiplier is applied.

> **Scoring Range:** An individual expedition's final score can range anywhere from **-80 to +156 points**.

---

## 6. Winning the Game
* Record the scores on paper, adding the current round's totals to previous rounds.
* Shuffle all cards for the next round. The player with the **most total points so far** begins play for the next round.
* After the agreed-upon number of rounds (typically three), the player with the **highest total score** wins the game!

---

## 7. Variant: Ruins (4-Player Partnership Game)

If you want to play with 4 players, you need **two game sets**.

### Setup
* Use the game components from one set, and add all cards with values **2, 3, and 4 from the second set** to the deck. The 4-player deck thus contains **75 cards** total.
* Players sitting opposite each other form partnerships and play on the **same side of the board**.

### Rules Amendments
* **Strictly Increasing Values:** When adding cards to an expedition, numbers must be strictly increasing. Two cards of the *same value* may not be played into the same expedition.
* **Partner Card Passing:** Instead of taking a normal turn (playing and drawing a card), a player may choose any **two of their cards and pass them face down to their partner**. 
  * *Hand Limit Restriction:* A player may never reduce their hand to **less than six cards** when passing.
* **Communication Ban:** Apart from passing cards, partners are **strictly prohibited** from communicating about their play during the game.
